# The Saurus — Landing Page Hero Illustration

## Target: Nano Banana

---

## Prompt

A hand-drawn scientific diagram showing the pipeline of a literature review tool, rendered as a whimsical process schematic on ruled notebook paper.

### Overall Composition
- Horizontal layout (landscape, ~16:9 or 3:2 aspect ratio)
- Three-act flow reading LEFT to RIGHT: **Input → Digestion → Synthesis**
- The dinosaur mascot is the CENTRAL element — the "processor" in the pipeline
- Background: faint horizontal ruled lines like a research notebook page (light gray, evenly spaced)
- A dashed horizontal baseline/timeline runs across the bottom connecting the three stages
- Below the baseline: small mono-spaced labels marking stages — "INPUT", "DIGESTION", "SYNTHESIS" (or numbered "01", "02", "03")
- The whole image feels like a figure from a biology textbook, annotated by a witty researcher

### LEFT: Input (Papers)
- 2-3 PDF document pages, slightly overlapping, tilted at casual angles
- Papers are mid-air, floating/flying TOWARD the dinosaur's open mouth
- Each paper has faint horizontal lines suggesting text content
- One paper has a small "PDF" label in the corner
- Dashed curved lines (trajectory arcs) showing the papers' path toward the mouth — like a physics diagram of projectile motion
- Papers should feel like they're being PULLED into the mouth, not thrown

### CENTER: The Dinosaur (Digestion)
- Friendly stegosaurus-like dinosaur, facing LEFT (toward the incoming papers)
- Mouth WIDE OPEN, actively receiving/chomping the nearest paper — paper halfway in the mouth
- Body is round, slightly chubby — friendly, not fierce, not muscular
- 3-5 small triangular dorsal plates along the spine
- Thick tail curves behind to the right
- Two sturdy short legs, standing on the baseline
- Small monocle on the visible eye (thin gold circle) — the academic touch
- Expression: eager, hungry, slightly comedic — one eyebrow raised, eyes focused on the papers
- Small comical details: tiny crumb-like paper scraps falling from the mouth, or a satisfied squint
- The dinosaur should look like it's ENJOYING the meal
- NO color fill on the body — line art only, same green ink as everything else
- Small annotation arrows or hatch marks on the belly suggesting "processing happening inside" (optional, scientific diagram style)

### RIGHT: Output (Review)
- A bound notebook/book, standing upright or slightly angled
- Dark green cover with "Review" written on it in cream/light text
- A gold bookmark ribbon hanging from the bottom
- Small checkmark or verification symbol near the book (gold)
- Small sparkle/radiance lines around the book suggesting "freshly produced"
- The book should feel like a FINISHED PRODUCT — polished, complete, in contrast to the messy papers on the left

### Connecting Elements
- Dashed or dotted line connecting the three stages along the bottom (timeline)
- Optional: small arrows or flow indicators between stages
- The flow should be unmistakable: papers go IN (left), dinosaur PROCESSES (center), review comes OUT (right)

### Style
- Hand-drawn ink illustration — like a field notebook sketch by a confident researcher
- Single-weight line art, consistent stroke (~2px), slightly organic/imperfect lines
- Monochrome dark academic green (#2D6A4F) for all lines and the dinosaur
- Papers: light gray outlines with faint text lines
- Gold (#D4AF37) accents ONLY on: monocle frame, bookmark ribbon, checkmark/sparkle on output book
- Labels in a mono-spaced or technical font style, muted gray
- NO heavy shading, NO gradients, NO color fills on the dinosaur
- The ruled notebook lines are very faint — background texture, not dominant

### Tone
- Scientific illustration meets editorial cartoon
- The humor is SUBTLE — a serious diagram format with a ridiculous subject (a dinosaur eating papers)
- Think: a figure you'd find in a Nature "News & Views" sidebar illustration
- Think: the kind of thing a professor would draw on a whiteboard, then students would photograph it
- NOT children's book. NOT corporate. NOT pixel art.
- The comedy comes from the contrast between the formal diagrammatic register (labels, baselines, annotations) and the absurd content (a dinosaur eating PDFs)

### What to AVOID
- Do NOT make the dinosaur look like a lizard, gecko, or crocodile — clearly a dinosaur (round body, dorsal plates, thick tail)
- Do NOT make it muscular or fierce — round, soft, scholarly
- Do NOT place the review book near the tail/rear end (reads as defecation)
- Do NOT use thick cartoon outlines or bubble style
- Do NOT add a landscape background (trees, sky, etc.) — this is a DIAGRAM, not a scene
- Do NOT make the dinosaur face right — must face LEFT
- Do NOT fill the dinosaur body with color — line art only
- Do NOT make it too busy — whitespace between the three stages is part of the design (editorial breathing)

### Size and Format
- Landscape orientation, approximately 2400x1200px or wider
- PNG with transparent background (or cream #FAFAF7)
- The illustration should work inside a hero section with text overlaid on top or beside it
- Must be legible at 50% scale (1200x600 on mobile)

---

## Caption (for placement under the illustration in the landing page)
`fig. 1 — digestion, schematic` (mono font, muted gray, centered)

---

## Context of Use
This illustration occupies the hero section of the landing page, beside or below the headline "Feed The Saurus your papers." It should communicate the entire product concept in a single glance: you give it papers, it processes them, you get a literature review. The diagram format adds credibility (this is a tool for researchers), while the dinosaur adds warmth and memorability.
