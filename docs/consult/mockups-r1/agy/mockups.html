In Vivo Hepatic Gene Correction: Delivery Kinetics, Editor Architectures, and the Translation Frontier

A comprehensive corpus synthesis of 40 papers (2022–2024) across 6 consolidated themes. Every finding addressed to paper, page, and paragraph.
Two primary delivery platforms, three editor architectures, and one pervasive physiological bottleneck: the liver remains the most accessible target for in vivo therapeutic genome editing, yet the field faces an intrinsic compromise between expression durability and vector immunogenicity.

1 · Delivery Modalities & Vector Kinetics

Recent advances in hepatic gene correction have converged on two primary delivery modalities: adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors and lipid nanoparticles (LNPs). AAV9-mediated delivery has demonstrated sustained therapeutic expression in murine models of metabolic liver disease [1](p.12,§3), while LNP-encapsulated base editors achieved transient but high-efficiency editing in hereditary tyrosinemia [2](p.8,§2). The choice between delivery platforms involves trade-offs between durability and immunogenicity [5](p.15,§4).

1.1 Recombinant AAV Vector Tropism

Across four studies dedicated to viral transduction, serotype-specific tropism dictated therapeutic ceiling. Recombinant AAV9 exhibited 60% hepatocyte transduction efficiency [1](p.12,§3), while split-intein dual-AAV constructs successfully reconstituted full-length large genes in 35% of hepatocytes [3](p.4,§1).

2 · Editor Architecture: Base Editors vs. Standard Nucleases

Comparative analysis illustrates that while canonical Cas9 nucleases retain broad versatility, adenine base editors (ABEs) provide superior therapeutic indexes in single-nucleotide metabolic disorders [5](p.15,§4). In vivo ABE administration rescued ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency, normalizing serum ammonia levels [4](p.9,§2).

• Methodological Conflict Detected Across Corpus
Rodriguez & Singh [5](p.15,§4) identified measurable translocations via unbiased GUIDE-seq. Conversely, Okafor et al. [4](p.9,§2) observed no alterations using targeted deep sequencing of 8 computationally anticipated sites. The Saurus flags this as an assay sensitivity discrepancy rather than genuine biological divergence.

1.2 Lipid Nanoparticles and Non-Viral Vectors

LNPs invert the traditional viral trade-off: avoiding capsid immunity permits repeat systemic administrations. LNP-delivered ABE8e corrected the splice-site mutation in 47% of hepatocytes [2](p.8,§2), and re-dosing was tolerated without transaminase elevation where AAV failed [2](p.14,§1).

Across the corpus, editing efficiency for LNP platforms clustered between 31% and 52%, measured by whole-liver lysate deep sequencing rather than sorted hepatocytes [2](p.9,§1) — a methodological nuance flagged by the assistant.
