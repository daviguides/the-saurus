Hepatic gene correction: delivery platforms, editor architectures, and the road to trials

Synthesized from 40 papers (2022–2024) · 6 themes · every claim addressed to paper, page, and paragraph

Two delivery platforms, three editor architectures, and one recurring bottleneck — the liver is the most tractable organ for in vivo editing, and also the one where durability and immunogenicity pull hardest against each other.

1 · Delivery modalities

Recent advances in hepatic gene correction have converged on two primary delivery modalities: adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors and lipid nanoparticles (LNPs). AAV9-mediated delivery has demonstrated sustained therapeutic expression in murine models of metabolic liver disease [1](p.12,§3), while LNP-encapsulated base editors achieved transient but high-efficiency editing in hereditary tyrosinemia [2](p.8,§2). The choice between delivery platforms involves trade-offs between durability and immunogenicity [5](p.15,§4), with emerging evidence suggesting that dual-AAV split-intein approaches may overcome payload limitations [3](p.4,§1).

Citation [1] · hovering
“AAV9 achieved 60% hepatocyte transduction efficiency at 1×10¹² vg/kg.”
Liu, W. et al. (2024) · Nature Gene Therapy · p.12 §3 · extracted claim c_0142 · open source PDF →

1.1 AAV vectors

Across four papers, serotype choice dominated transduction efficiency more than dose. AAV9 reached 60% hepatocyte transduction at 1×10¹² vg/kg [1](p.12,§3), whereas engineered capsids from library screens exceeded this only marginally in the same dose range [9](p.6,§2). Durability remains the open question: episomal genomes dilute in dividing hepatocytes, an effect pronounced in juvenile models [10](p.11,§5).

No paper in this corpus reports durability beyond 52 weeks in a dividing-liver model. Treat long-term claims as extrapolation.

1.2 Lipid nanoparticles

LNPs invert the trade-off — no capsid immunity, no integration risk, but a single transient exposure. LNP-delivered ABE8e corrected the splice-site mutation in 47% of hepatocytes [2](p.8,§2), and re-dosing was tolerated where AAV re-administration was not [2](p.14,§1).

2 · Editor architecture

Where payload exceeds AAV capacity, split-intein reconstitution restored full-length ATP7B protein in 35% of treated cells [3](p.4,§1). Base editors avoid double-strand breaks entirely: in vivo adenine base editing rescued ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency with survival benefit at 12 weeks [4](p.9,§2). Comparative work favors base editors on safety but nucleases on versatility [5](p.15,§4).

Two do, and the disagreement is methodological rather than substantive.

Rodriguez & Singh report measurable indels at predicted off-target sites for SpCas9 in hepatocytes [5](p.15,§4). Okafor et al. found none detectable with adenine base editing [4](p.9,§2) — but they assayed only the top 8 computationally predicted sites, while [5] ran genome-wide GUIDE-seq [5](p.16,§1).

So the corpus does not establish that base editors are cleaner; it establishes that they were tested less exhaustively.
